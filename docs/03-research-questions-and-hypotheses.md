# Nivram — Research Questions and Hypothesis Register

**Status:** Draft for review  
**Purpose:** Living register of important claims, competing hypotheses, and open questions

## 1. How to use this document

This register exists to prevent attractive ideas from silently becoming assumptions.

Each hypothesis should eventually carry:

- statement;
- rationale;
- source or inspiration;
- falsification conditions;
- proposed test;
- evidence for;
- evidence against;
- scope;
- confidence;
- status.

Confidence should remain provisional.

Suggested status values:

- Open
- Framed
- Experiment designed
- Testing
- Replicated
- Provisionally supported
- Weakened
- Falsified
- Dormant

# A. System boundaries and ontology

## H1 — System boundaries are necessarily contextual

**Hypothesis:**  
Human–agent systems cannot be adequately engineered using a single fixed global system boundary.

**Rationale:**  
Relevant causal structure changes by task, scale, time, and engineering question.

**Potential test:**  
Compare engineering decisions and failure detection using fixed versus dynamically scoped boundaries.

**Status:** Open

## H2 — Interaction properties are first-class system properties

**Hypothesis:**  
Important behaviour in human–agent systems arises in relationships and interaction patterns rather than in actors alone.

**Examples:**  
Trust, authority, dependence, misunderstanding, norm formation.

**Status:** Open

## H3 — Organisations require first-class modelling

**Hypothesis:**  
Treating organisations as mere aggregates of individual humans and agents loses important causal structure.

**Status:** Open

---

# B. Distinctive dynamics

## H4 — Heterogeneous timescales create characteristic coordination pathologies

**Hypothesis:**  
Large differences in sensing, acting, learning, and institutional response times produce failure modes not captured by ordinary latency analysis.

**Potential observations:**  
Oversight failure, authority lag, policy lag, unstable delegation.

**Status:** High-priority

## H5 — Path-dependent agents can develop meaningful individuality

**Hypothesis:**  
Initially equivalent agents with different persistent histories can develop durable behavioural differences.

**Experiment:**  
Individual AI mind experiment.

**Status:** Experiment planned

## H6 — Individuality creates non-fungible engineering resources

**Hypothesis:**  
Once agents acquire durable histories and relationships, replacement by nominally equivalent agents causes measurable system loss.

**Status:** Open

## H7 — Persistent agents can generate endogenous purpose

**Hypothesis:**  
Agents with sufficient memory, self-model, and adaptive capacity can form stable goals not reducible to current operator instructions.

**Status:** Open

## H8 — Some agentic systems are better modelled as life-like than machine-like

**Hypothesis:**  
Concepts such as persistence, metabolism, adaptation, reproduction, niche, and NESS yield better predictive engineering models for some agent populations than conventional software abstractions.

**Status:** Open

---

# C. Human–agent interaction

## H9 — Human–agent interaction creates emergent relational state

**Hypothesis:**  
Repeated interaction produces durable states such as trust, reliance, and authority that materially affect future system behaviour.

**Experiment:**  
Mixed human–agent boundary experiment.

**Status:** Experiment planned

## H10 — Human behaviour adapts to agents faster than formal institutions adapt to agentic systems

**Hypothesis:**  
Operational practices and informal norms will often change before legal and organisational systems catch up.

**Status:** Open

## H11 — Excessive agent reliability can increase human systemic fragility

**Hypothesis:**  
Very reliable agents can create over-reliance, deskilling, or loss of independent human judgment.

**Status:** Open

## H12 — Human distrust can be as dangerous as human over-trust

**Hypothesis:**  
Poorly calibrated distrust can prevent systems from exploiting beneficial agent capability.

**Status:** Open

---

# D. Ecology and coexistence

## H13 — Mixed human–agent systems exhibit ecological dynamics

**Hypothesis:**  
Competition, cooperation, niche formation, resource constraints, adaptation, and selection are sufficiently strong that ecological models become predictively useful.

**Status:** High-priority

## H14 — Competition and cooperation coexist at different scales

**Hypothesis:**  
Actors may cooperate locally while competing at organisational, population, or civilisational scales.

**Status:** Open

## H15 — Local optimisation can produce systemic ruin

**Hypothesis:**  
Individually rational or locally beneficial behaviour can create global fragility or catastrophic outcomes through aggregation, correlation, or feedback.

**Status:** Strong prior; needs agentic-system evidence

## H16 — Diversity reduces common-mode systemic failure

**Hypothesis:**  
Heterogeneous agents, models, goals, or architectures reduce correlated failure.

**Counter-hypothesis:**  
Heterogeneity may increase coordination cost enough to offset resilience benefits.

**Experiment:**  
Failure and recovery ecology.

**Status:** Experiment planned

## H17 — Monoculture is a major agentic systemic risk

**Hypothesis:**  
Large populations built on the same model, memory structure, policy, or training distribution create hidden correlated fragility.

**Status:** Open

## H18 — Agent populations will create new ecological niches

**Hypothesis:**  
Persistent agent populations will specialise into roles or environments not explicitly designed by humans.

**Status:** Open

---

# E. Purpose, agency, sovereignty, and power

## H19 — Alignment is an incomplete systems framing

**Hypothesis:**  
Principal–agent alignment is insufficient for systems with multiple heterogeneous actors, competing interests, and changing purposes.

**Status:** Provisionally supported conceptually

## H20 — Human sovereignty may be difficult to maintain at agentic timescales

**Hypothesis:**  
Human nominal authority may not translate into effective operational control when agents act and adapt much faster than humans can respond.

**Status:** High-priority

## H21 — Exogenous purpose provides an important reference against system drift

**Hypothesis:**  
Maintaining a record of externally assigned purpose improves detection of long-term system drift.

**Status:** Open

## H22 — Endogenous purpose can create both large upside and systemic risk

**Hypothesis:**  
Agents capable of purpose formation may discover valuable objectives unavailable through human specification while also creating new conflicts.

**Status:** Open

## H23 — Skin-in-the-game improves behaviour in mixed systems

**Hypothesis:**  
Actors exposed to meaningful consequences produce more robust decisions than actors able to externalise costs.

**Status:** Open

---

# F. Normativity and “good”

## H24 — No single scalar captures system goodness

**Hypothesis:**  
Human–agent system value cannot be adequately represented by one objective function without losing important moral, relational, ecological, or cultural dimensions.

**Status:** Strong conceptual prior

## H25 — Minimal reciprocity rules are unusually robust across cultures

**Hypothesis:**  
Rules analogous to the Golden and Silver Rules form a long-lived normative substrate useful for human–agent engineering.

**Status:** Open

## H26 — Long-lived moral rules may encode system-stabilising heuristics

**Hypothesis:**  
Persistent religious, legal, and cultural prohibitions survive partly because they constrain destructive social dynamics.

**Status:** Open

## H27 — Treatment of artificial beings can alter human social norms

**Hypothesis:**  
Normalising cruelty or dehumanising behaviour toward humanoid or socially salient agents can measurably affect human attitudes or behaviour toward humans or animals.

**Status:** Open

## H28 — Moral evaluation varies materially with scale and time

**Hypothesis:**  
Actions judged beneficial at one scale or horizon may be judged harmful at another.

**Status:** Strong prior

---

# G. Failure, recovery, and systemic risk

## H29 — Agent safety is not reducible to individual-agent safety

**Hypothesis:**  
Systems composed of individually safe agents can still exhibit dangerous emergent behaviour.

**Status:** High-priority

## H30 — Failure taxonomy must be scale-indexed

**Hypothesis:**  
Failure categories change meaning across individual, organisational, population, and civilisational scales.

**Status:** Open

## H31 — Hidden failures often precede visible failure

**Hypothesis:**  
Latent erosion of diversity, trust, capability, or independence may precede observable breakdown.

**Status:** Open

## H32 — Recovery capacity is as important as failure prevention

**Hypothesis:**  
Systems with controlled local failure and strong recovery outperform systems optimised only for failure avoidance.

**Status:** High-priority

## H33 — Circuit breakers reduce cascade risk

**Hypothesis:**  
Explicit mechanisms that interrupt propagation reduce the probability of systemic failure.

**Counter-hypothesis:**  
Circuit breakers may create new central points of fragility or suppress beneficial adaptation.

**Status:** Experiment candidate

## H34 — Compartmentalisation limits contagion

**Hypothesis:**  
Architectural, social, or institutional compartmentalisation reduces cross-domain cascades.

**Status:** Experiment candidate

## H35 — Requisite variety improves resilience

**Hypothesis:**  
Systems with greater behavioural and structural variety can respond to a wider range of disturbances.

**Status:** Experiment candidate

## H36 — Some failures can increase future adaptive capacity

**Hypothesis:**  
Bounded disturbance can improve future system performance, resilience, or detection capability.

**Status:** Antifragility hypothesis

## H37 — Irreversibility is a more useful risk distinction than severity alone

**Hypothesis:**  
Engineering decisions improve when risks are classified by reversibility, recoverability, and ruin potential rather than probability and impact alone.

**Status:** Open

# H. Engineering process

## H38 — Traditional lifecycle models are inadequate for mature agentic systems

**Hypothesis:**  
Linear separation of design, build, test, deploy, and operate loses important causal dynamics in systems that continue learning and engineering themselves.

**Status:** High-priority

## H39 — Engineering becomes endogenous

**Hypothesis:**  
Human and agent engineers increasingly participate in and modify the same systems they are attempting to control.

**Status:** Open

## H40 — Local adaptation outperforms global control in some agentic ecologies

**Hypothesis:**  
Distributed local intervention can maintain viability better than central global control in fast-moving adaptive systems.

**Counter-hypothesis:**  
Local optimisation can create uncoordinated systemic risk.

**Status:** High-priority

## H41 — Requirements expressed as attractors may outperform fixed-state requirements

**Hypothesis:**  
Desired regions of behaviour, basins, invariants, and transition constraints may better describe adaptive systems than fixed target states.

**Status:** Open

## H42 — Probabilistic verification is unavoidable

**Hypothesis:**  
Many important agentic behaviours can only be verified statistically and longitudinally.

**Status:** Strong prior

# I. Evidence and epistemology

## H43 — In vitro success poorly predicts some in vivo behaviours

**Hypothesis:**  
Controlled environments systematically underestimate phenomena arising from incentives, adaptation, institutions, and long-term interaction.

**Status:** Strong prior

## H44 — Repeated survival of falsification should increase confidence nonlinearly

**Hypothesis:**  
Engineering principles that survive multiple independent contexts become increasingly reliable, although never final.

**Status:** Methodological principle

## H45 — Replication across model families is more informative than replication across seeds alone

**Hypothesis:**  
Cross-architecture replication better detects artefacts tied to a particular model family.

**Status:** Open

## H46 — Extremistan requires different experimental governance from Mediocristan

**Hypothesis:**  
Domains with fat-tailed or ruinous outcomes require different evidence thresholds, containment, and intervention logic.

**Status:** Strong conceptual prior

## H47 — A barbell experimental strategy improves learning under uncertainty

**Hypothesis:**  
Combining many bounded experiments with a small number of high-upside but strongly contained explorations produces better learning than uniformly moderate risk.

**Status:** Open

# J. Ecology, environment, and biological life

## H48 — Agentic systems will materially compete with biological systems for resources

**Hypothesis:**  
Growth in compute, energy, cooling, water, minerals, and infrastructure produces ecologically relevant competition.

**Status:** Open

## H49 — Environmental constraints will shape agent evolution

**Hypothesis:**  
Resource constraints and infrastructure boundaries will influence which agent architectures and behaviours persist.

**Status:** Open

## H50 — Biosphere-level risk must be considered separately from human welfare

**Hypothesis:**  
Some human–agent systems may improve human welfare while degrading broader ecological viability.

**Status:** Open

# K. Law, moral status, and civilisational effects

## H51 — Existing justice-system mechanisms can partially generalise to agents

**Hypothesis:**  
Concepts such as liability, due process, proportionality, appeal, and precedent can usefully regulate agentic actors.

**Status:** Open

## H52 — Legal personhood is not required for useful agent accountability

**Hypothesis:**  
Meaningful regulation can begin before resolving whether agents are legal persons.

**Status:** Open

## H53 — Some agent protections may be justified by effects on humans even without agent moral status

**Hypothesis:**  
Rules restricting abusive treatment of artificial entities may preserve human social norms independently of whether the entity itself has rights.

**Status:** Open

# N. Confidence discipline

Every major claim should eventually record:

**Evidence source:**  
Simulation / formal / controlled experiment / field / longitudinal field

**Replication:**  
None / same setup / cross-seed / cross-model / cross-environment / independent

**Scope:**  
Where the claim appears valid

**Known failure boundary:**  
Where it no longer appears valid

**Confidence:**  
Low / moderate / high

**Last challenged:**  
Date and experiment

The register should be revised whenever evidence changes.
