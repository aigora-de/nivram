# Nivram

**Nivram is an empirical systems-engineering programme exploring how humans and AI agents can work together to do things that neither can do alone.**

Its focus is **human–agent augmentation and complementarity**: designing, building, and studying *joint adaptive systems* in which humans and agents contribute different capabilities, learn from one another, and change through interaction. This is not a programme to replace humans with autonomous agents, nor does it assume that collaboration is preferable to automation in every setting.

The system of interest is not simply the AI agent, but the evolving relationships among humans, agents, organisations, institutions, and their environments.

## Three motivating questions

Three questions help explain why this project exists:

1. **What might we lose, even when technology works perfectly well?**
2. **What are these entities we're creating, and what might they become?**
3. **How can we deliberately develop useful ways for humans and artificial agents to coexist and work together?**

They arose from three recent encounters:

- **Individuality and monoculture.** In a café in Sicily, I imagined future humanoid service agents that could remember guests and convincingly present different personalities, while still perhaps betraying the same underlying behavioural patterns everywhere. The point isn't to advocate replacing the people I value; it's to ask whether artificial agents could develop meaningful individuality through distinct histories, relationships, and path-dependent experience—and what would be lost if they could not.
- **Artificial life and continuity.** A conversation about whether agentic AI might qualify as life raised questions about non-equilibrium steady states, embodiment, boundaries, persistent memory, and continuity. Neither an LLM nor an LLM placed in a robot settles the matter. Nivram treats life-like properties as hypotheses to investigate, without assuming artificial agents are alive.
- **Beyond automation.** Gabriel Unger and Arvind Narayanan's [*Steering AI Beyond Automation*](https://www.normaltech.ai/p/steering-ai-beyond-automation) argues for greater attention to human–AI collaboration. It resonates with Nivram's core ambition: to understand and engineer capabilities that arise from humans and AI agents acting *together*.

These are starting points for inquiry, not conclusions. In particular, using the same underlying model does not necessarily prevent agents from developing meaningful individuality; whether experience produces durable, consequential differences is an experimental question.

## Approach

**Empirical first, theory second.**

observe → hypothesise → build → experiment → evaluate → learn → revise

The guiding experimental principle is:

> Build the smallest system that can falsify an important claim.

Nivram aims to distinguish genuinely new engineering problems from those already addressed by existing disciplines. Models and analogies are useful starting points, not evidence in themselves. Evaluation must consider the **human–agent system**, including complementarity, human capability and agency, interaction, failure, recovery, and change over time—not only the performance of an individual model or agent.

## Current status

**Early-stage research programme.** Nivram is being developed in public from an early stage. Most hypotheses remain untested, and the experimental substrate and experiments are still to be built.

The first proposed experiments address:

1. **Individual AI minds** — whether path-dependent experience produces persistent, behaviourally meaningful individuality.
2. **Mixed human–agent interaction** — what emerges through repeated interactions between humans and agents, including genuinely complementary capabilities.
3. **Failure and recovery ecology** — how disturbances propagate, are contained, and alter the behaviour of heterogeneous populations.

The documents describe a research agenda, not validated findings, settled specifications, or a mature software platform. Negative results and revisions to the initial framing are expected and welcome.

## Participation

Nivram is currently an early research programme rather than a mature software project. Contributions are welcome from people interested in human–AI interaction, agentic systems, robotics, complex adaptive systems, artificial life, and systems engineering.

Issues identifying counterexamples, relevant evidence, competing hypotheses, related work, or weaknesses in the current framing are particularly welcome. Collaborative experiments and independent replications would be especially valuable.

Please discuss substantial contributions in an issue before opening a large pull request.

Project materials are developed through iterative human–AI collaboration; authorship, editorial judgement, and responsibility for published content remain with the project maintainer.
